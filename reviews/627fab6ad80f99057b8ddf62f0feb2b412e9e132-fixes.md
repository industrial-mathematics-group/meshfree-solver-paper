## Findings

### Should-fix

**N1. `paper/main_light.tex:512`: "5 to 30 times smaller … at the finest resolution" is still wrong in 1-D.**
- The fix took the wording of paper pass 2 (P11). It did not take the 1-D remark of pass 1 (`:512` minor).
- On 1-D lattices at N = 3163, `results/reports/2026-10-29-rerun-078-tables.md` gives these R = 5.5 / R = 2.5
  error ratios: K = 2: 1.05e-3 / 2.10e-4 = **5.0**; K = 4: 2.06e-8 / 4.94e-10 = **42**.
- In 2-D the ratios are 4.9 / 11.4 / 30.7 (advection) and 5.0 / 11.2 / 31.7 (Burgers).
- The sentence names only "K = 3 in one dimension" as the exception, so as written it claims 5–30 for 1-D K = 4 as well.
- Suggested wording: "in two dimensions … 5 to 30 times smaller at the finest resolution (42 times for K = 4 in one
  dimension)". Alternatively, "5 to 40". `:410` is fine, since it already says "on two-dimensional lattices".

**N2. `paper/main_light.tex:620` does not match the strict safeguard of PR #11.**
- The sentence reads: "requires 1.5·6 = 9 neighbours …; at θ = 0.4 and κ = 5.5 some stencils have fewer".
- With the rule now stated at `:246` ("more than 1.5 times") the fit needs **more than** 9 neighbours.
- No stencil has fewer than 9. The minimum is exactly 9, and 10.3 % of the particles have 9 (Monte Carlo on 2·10⁵
  particles, `neighbours_k5_1d.py`; the same as `nb.py` of paper pass 1).
- So the explanation holds, as `CHANGES.md` says, but the sentence still describes the non-strict rule.
- Suggested wording: "requires more than 1.5·6 = 9 neighbours; at θ = 0.4 and κ = 5.5 about a tenth of the stencils
  have only nine, fall back to degree four …".
- The other places that use the rule are consistent with the strict form:
  - `:246`, "κ ≥ 5.5 in 1-D … on lattices κ = 5 and κ ≈ 3.2": a lattice at κ = 5 has 10 > 9 neighbours, and at κ = 3.2 in 2-D it has 36 > 31.5.
  - The degree-three increase on the perturbed 2-D clouds: 16 > 15.
  - Addendum `:264`.

### Minor

**N3. The DMR figure readings sit about 0.011 downstream of the exact incident shock.**
- I compared the incident shock with the exact line x = 1/6 + (y + 4)/√3 at three heights in all four panels. I used the
  frame detection of `frames.py` and a half-jump criterion (`dmr_incident_offset.py`):

  | y | exact | read |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.97 | 3.036 | 3.046–3.049 |
  | 0.90 | 2.996 | 3.005–3.008 |
  | 0.80 | 2.938 | 2.947–2.950 |

- `dmrpos.py`'s own criterion gives the same values, 3.046–3.049 at y = 0.97.
- `CHANGES.md` lists "incident 3.047–3.049" as expected, without noting that the exact value at y = 0.97 is 3.036
  (3.053 is the value at y = 1).
- The offset is the same at every height, so it is an offset of the reading, not of the solution. The Riemann readings
  show no offset.
- Consequence: the DMR stem positions (≈ 2.76, foot 2.79) may be about 0.01 too far right. This is at the limit of
  the stated accuracy of "about 0.01" (`:418`), and no conclusion depends on it. Worth a look at the DMR panel
  geometry or the x-range 0–3.2 if these positions are ever tightened.

**N4. Addendum `rem:lean` (`:103`) lists "(H3)" among the ingredients that Lean takes as hypotheses for `thm:mood`.**
(H3) is already a hypothesis of the theorem itself, so listing it next to the real gaps (`hSO`, exact locality, `hone`)
makes it look like a gap. Cosmetic.

**N5. Stability note, `tab:measured` (`:168`) and `obs:deformed(ii)` (`:1102`).** These say "K_V − 1 < 5·10⁻⁴ (2-D)".
`tables/ho_deformed.tex` prints `5e-4` for K = 2, n = 12, which is 4.8·10⁻⁴ rounded. A reader sees "< 5e-4" next to a
table entry 5e-4. "≤ 5·10⁻⁴", or printing two digits, would avoid this. Cosmetic.
